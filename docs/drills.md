## Reading the numbers

**App-service-outage ~4m 10s** is dominated by `step_scale_standby` (1 → 3
instances). The standby is deliberately kept at one instance to save money; the
price of that saving is ~2 minutes of RTO. The 412s outlier on 2 Dec was a cold
App Service plan (scale-from-zero on the standby plan after a config change).

**SQL failure ~2m 40s** is faster than an app-tier failure because promotion is
a control-plane operation, not a scale operation. The measured RPO gap at
promotion was 3–6 seconds across all drills.

**Front Door origin disable ~1m 05s** is the fastest because there is nothing
to promote — the orchestrator only has to flip origin priorities. This is why
the probe interval (15s) and the grace period (60s) are the whole RTO in that
case.

## What would make these numbers worse

- **Longer grace period.** We use 60s. A 5-minute grace would add 4 minutes to
  every RTO and would have prevented zero of these drills from passing.
- **Probe interval > 15s.** The AFD probe is the first detector; every second
  there is a second of RTO.
- **Bigger standby scale-out.** Going 1 → 6 instances instead of 1 → 3 adds
  roughly 90s. Match standby size to the traffic you actually need to serve
  during an incident, not to your steady-state capacity.
